Abstract
We study synchronous majority dynamics on the infinite square lattice. Each site reads its
four nearest neighbours, without reading its own state, and resolves a tie with an independent
fair coin. Starting from a finite perturbation of either homogeneous configuration, measure the
perturbation by the side length of its smallest translated axis-parallel enclosing square. The
worst extended expected recovery time among deterministic initial configurations of size exactly
𝑁 satisfies
𝑁 2
576000000 ≤ 𝜏 (𝑁 ) ≤ 664066𝑁 2 (𝑁 ≥ 500001).
The upper bound alone holds for 𝑁 ≥ 16 , with a uniform geometric bound on its block tails.
Retaining the block failure rate gives recovery by an explicit quadratic deadline with probability
tending to one, as well as bounds on all fixed positive moments and a common interval of
exponential moments. The filled square attains the worst extended expectation for every 𝑁 ≥ 2 .
This is the quadratic order conjectured in Question 7.4 of Fatès, Marcovici and Taati. A
weighted loss estimate supplies the lower bound. A staircase boundary, finite exclusion moments,
and a local-clearing event supply the upper bound. An explicit comparison transfers estimates
from marked-clock physical time to synchronous rounds. The proof is organized into six lemmas:
normalization, clock/synchronous comparison, weighted slow extinction, mixed local clearing,

Question 7.4 conjectures the quadratic order in Theorem 1. A limited public-source search on
28 October 2026 did not identify an earlier proof for the same model. That search is not a
certification of global priority or exhaustive coverage, and an unchanged arXiv version does not
establish the problem’s present public status.
A related two-dimensional zero-temperature Ising model already has quadratic extinction esti-
mates [CMST10, Theorem 2]. Its sites update at independent rate-one Poisson clocks; [FMT22,
Example 4.2] explicitly distinguishes it from the synchronous model. Physical clock time cannot
simply be renamed as a number of parallel rounds.
The proof uses established ideas. Peres and Winkler [PW11] develop monotone-system censoring
and comparisons of update schemes. Liu and Yin [LY24, Lemma 3.11] give Poisson-schedule
dependency-depth bounds, crediting earlier work. The scope of this draft is the original-model
quadratic theorem and its formal artifact. It makes no claim that censoring, exclusion encodings,
heat estimates, or clock-chain bounds were invented in this project.

3.1. Checkerboard splitting

exactly 1/2, with no additional factor for its two directions. There are no exchanges beyond the
endpoints and no particle creation or removal. Equation (E4.7) is the finite symmetric exclusion
update generator with this rate convention.
The calculation identifies the original marked update operator. It does not, by itself, establish
a stochastic-law identification or the moment evolution used in Lemma 4. The moment argu-
ment that follows derives the expectation equations directly from the original independent gap
and mark fields, using (E4.7) for their drift. This keeps the deterministic encoding and the
probabilistic evolution as separate steps.

6.2. Perimeter pays for every cleanup deletion
The initial boundary contains 8𝐿 + 4 unit edges. Every cleanup deletion consumes at least two
of these perimeter units, and a raw majority update never replenishes them. Therefore there
are at most 4𝐿 + 2 cleanup deletion occurrences. Each has weight at most 𝐿 + 2.

protection. We then show, under the original clock law, that the expected accumulated weighted
loss equals the time integral of the actual weighted cleanup rate. The resulting bound (𝐿 +
2)(4𝐿 + 2) holds for every physical horizon 𝑇 ≥ 0 . The clock argument is needed: a bound on
a finite-word loss alone does not identify its expected rate.

8. Scope of the result
The lower argument needs a positive probability of surviving a quadratic number of rounds.
The upper argument needs a uniform positive probability of clearing within a quadratic block.
First and second moments suﬀice for the upper argument because the staircase geometry turns
a particle-count threshold into a square of white sites.
The constants in (4) are not optimized. No leading constant, limiting extinction law, shape
theorem, or stability from random perturbations is claimed. The retained failure rate gives
(4c); Theorem 4 supplies fixed positive moments and exponential integrability on its stated
interval. The filled square attains the exact worst extended expectation by (6a). The lower
argument gives a positive survival probability for that witness; the geometric upper tail (4a)
